## Notes — confirms the brief's warning
[confirmed — primary source] Pixabay's own license-summary page confirms the brief's premise: **the Pixabay Content License is NOT CC0.** It differs from CC0 in at least two material ways:
1. It is a **custom proprietary license** ("Pixabay License" / "Content License"), not a Creative Commons license at all — no CC0 dedication is claimed anywhere on this page.
2. It carries a **standalone-distribution restriction** — you cannot redistribute/resell the raw audio file itself as a standalone asset; it must be incorporated into a larger work. For a portfolio site embedding it as a background ambience track (not offering the file for download/resale), this restriction is very likely satisfied, but it is a real legal distinction from public-domain/CC0 that a redistribution-sensitive use case must respect.
3. No attribution required, which is CC0-like, but the "Content" is still explicitly stated to be under copyright — Pixabay's own FAQ (not separately fetched here) reportedly describes it as "protected by copyright but offered under a royalty-free license," i.e., a license grant, not a public-domain dedication. ## Recommendation impact
Because the brief's goal is explicitly "zero-licensing-risk," and Pixabay imposes a standalone-redistribution condition plus is not a CC0/public-domain dedication, Pixabay audio is a **usable but non-zero-risk** option — CC0 Freesound files or procedural synthesis carry strictly lower legal surface area.
